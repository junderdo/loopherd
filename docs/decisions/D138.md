# D138

## Decision

`view.layout` in `config.toml` picks how the herd view and the shepherd
share a tab (LH-410): `side-by-side` (the default when unset) puts the
shepherd left and the view right; `stacked` puts the view on top and the
shepherd below.

- **Both paths follow it.** The `view-beside-shepherd` action splits the
  view right of the shepherd's pane, or above it; `S` in the view starts a
  missing shepherd left of the view, or below it. The fallbacks (the
  focused pane, then the popup) split the same way.
- **Size.** The view takes 40% of the split's width or height either way.
- **Placing left or above.** herdr splits only right or down, so the new
  pane opens on the far side, then `pane swap` trades it with its target.
  A plugin pane opens at half and is resized by growing its target toward
  it; a shell pane opens at its share with `--ratio`.
- **Focus.** herdr's swap focuses its source pane and brings its tab
  forward, so the swap's source is the pane that should hold focus, and a
  pane the human had focused elsewhere in the tab is focused again by ID
  over herdr's socket (`pane.focus`), the CLI's `pane focus` only moving to
  a neighbor (D180). That refocus is best effort, and when herdr can't say which
  tab the human sees, a stacked view's swap can bring the shepherd's tab
  forward.
- **Malformed.** Any other value falls back to `side-by-side`, and the view
  shows a note, as a bad `view.theme` does. `config set` refuses it.

## Why

Each path had one fixed orientation, so the layout depended on which was
opened first. The human first picked stacked as the default, then on
2026-11-21 switched it to side by side. A key in the view to switch layouts
and the detail panel stacking at narrow widths are their own cards.

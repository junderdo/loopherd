# D116

## Decision

Amends D60 and D17 (LH-364, grilled with the human 2026-11-21). Unready
work gets groomed before a herd's slots empty, by a member rather than the
lead's context, and a reviewer's follow-ups no longer wait on a "mark
ready?" round-trip.

- **`[queue] low_at = <n>`**, default `2 × max`. A lead's watch prints
  `running low` when claimable is at or below `low_at` and unready is above
  0; its streak ends when claimable rises above `low_at` or unready reaches
  0. `low_at` below `max` is an error, reported by `loopherd herds`, `feed`
  and `watch` as for a bad template list. D80's `no workers open` form keeps
  its 0-claimable condition. For an unled herd, the shepherd acts when
  `status` shows claimable at or below `low_at`.
- **`follow_ups_ready`**, a seventh `[lead]` switch, default off, settable
  in `[lead_defaults]` and shown by `loopherd herds` like the others. It is
  prompt-only, as `new_cards` is, and applies only with `new_cards` on.
  When on, the lead files a follow-up of a PR it verified with
  `ready-for-agent` and `blocked`, `blocked-by: LH-<parent>`, so it becomes
  claimable once the parent card reaches Done, comments `filed ready:
  follow-up of PR <n>` on it, and says so in a `note`. A card qualifies
  only if it holds findings from the parent PR's review files, under the
  parent card's scope. Its criteria may offer the worker alternatives ("fix
  X, or D<n> records why not"). It doesn't qualify if any criterion needs
  the human's decision, changes a decision's direction, or carries a
  finding the reviewer called out of scope or design. Anything else is
  filed as now. The human vetoes by telling the shepherd, before the
  parent merges or by retiring the worker after.
- **A template field `slot = false`.** A member of such a template holds no
  `max` slot in `feed`, `add` or the claimable count. It is not a worker
  for `no workers open`, `out of work`, `drained` or `all drained`. It
  still gets its own `done`, stop and `gone` events. `attended = true`
  (D112) implies it.
- **A `groom` member.** A herd may define `[template.groom]`: name
  `groom-{herd}`, a detached worktree at origin's default branch, `slot =
  false`, no `done` or `merged` check, left out of `[queue] templates`.
  - **Trigger.** On `running low`, either form, a lead whose herd has the
    template runs `loopherd add <herd> groom` in place of D60's subagent
    triage, then ends its turn. It does nothing while `groom-<herd>` is
    open. The shepherd does the same for an unled herd. A herd without the
    template triages as D60 says.
  - **Rights.** It may edit card text, comment, mark duplicates by comment,
    check staleness against the code, and post a draft grill prompt as a
    comment on each grilling card. It never adds `ready-for-agent`,
    archives, moves a card between lists, or touches `need-human` or
    `blocked`. For a `need-human` card it only restates the open question
    in its report. These rights live in its prompt; it runs as any worker
    does until the worker sandbox exists (D107).
  - **Report.** One `done` report: D60's categories, the edits it made,
    and a numbered batch of proposed actions, each with the exact command
    that applies it (ready, archive with a reason, needs-spec comment). The
    lead relays it as one `question` and retires the groomer with
    `--close`.
  - **Applying it.** The human approves the batch, or all but some lines.
    The shepherd runs the approved lines and nothing else. Marking ready
    and archiving stay the shepherd's, on the human's word.
  - **Prompt.** The skill gets a Groom section with these rules; the
    example herd file's `[template.groom]` points at it. `loopherd init`
    doesn't scaffold the template yet.

This amends D60's threshold and its "only the shepherd marks a task ready;
no lead switch covers it", and adds a switch to D17's list. D61's
claimable count is unchanged.

Built as LH-375 (`low_at`), LH-376 (`follow_ups_ready`), LH-377 (`slot =
false`) and LH-378 (the groom member, after LH-377).
The skill, which assumes no tracker, writes the line as `blocked-by:
<parent key>` in the tracker's key form, `LH-<parent>` on this repo's
board. It files the follow-up before the parent merges, so the after-merge
unblock frees it, or unblocks right after filing when the parent is
already Done (LH-441).

## Why

On 2026-11-20 and 21 most of `loopherd-cards`' unready cards were the
lead's own review follow-ups (LH-348, 349, 354, 355, 356), whose criteria
a reviewer had written under scope the human had already approved; each
still cost a "mark ready?" round-trip. The lead triaging them hit its
context limit twice that day.

- **Ready on file, with a veto.** The human's say over scope came when
  they approved the parent card. Blocking the follow-up on its parent
  keeps an abandoned PR's follow-ups from running. `loopherd-cards` has
  `merge = true`, so no merge question could carry the veto; a `note`
  keeps it visible without waking anyone. LH-357, which carries a decision
  the human made, wouldn't qualify, and LH-346, which the human held to
  fold into LH-326, shows what the veto is for.
- **Prompt-only switch.** The tool hook only catches an honest lead's
  slips, and today no code stops a lead adding `ready-for-agent` anyway;
  it matches `new_cards`.
- **A member, not a subagent.** A fresh agent spends none of the lead's
  context and can take its time checking cards against the code. The same
  herd, because a lead's broker adds only its own herd's templates; one at
  a time, by its fixed name.
- **No slot.** At `2 × max` the herd's slots are usually full, so a
  groomer counted against `max` would hold a code slot and warn on every
  start. A field, not a special template name, and D112's attended
  members need the same rule.
- **Proposals, not archives.** Archiving says the work no longer matters,
  which is a scope call; text edits and comments aren't.
- **Earlier trigger.** At `max`, grooming started as slots emptied and
  landed after they sat idle. `low_at` below `max` would bring that back.
- **Command lines in the batch.** The shepherd runs what the human
  approved, line by line, with nothing to reinterpret. A `scripts/trello
  apply` can come if this proves error-prone.

Out of scope: hook enforcement of `follow_ups_ready` or of the groomer's
rights, `loopherd init` scaffolding, and the groomer archiving.

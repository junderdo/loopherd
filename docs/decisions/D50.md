# D50

## Decision

The lead hook counts a pane as a lead's while the ledger records a lead
there, retired or not, so a retired lead whose agent still runs keeps every
gate. It also always denies, in a lead's pane, `loopherd retire` of a name
starting `lead-`, of no name, or of a name bash may expand (`$`, a
backtick, a glob or a brace), through the same wrappers, `cargo run` forms
and cargo aliases as its other denials. Retiring a lead is the shepherd's
or the human's job; a lead still retires its members by name.

## Why

Matching only unretired leads let a lead run `loopherd retire lead-<herd>`
in its own pane and then run everything its switches deny. Matching a
retired lead on the same pane and socket, rather than failing closed for
any pane with a retired entry, is exact: herdr never reuses a pane id, so a
command from that pane comes from the lead's agent. The command text can't
say which lead a name belongs to beyond the `lead-<herd>` naming every lead
gets, so the hook denies retiring any lead, and an unreadable name, rather
than reading the ledger to match its own name.

A lead respawned under the same name replaces the retired record, so a
retired lead's agent left running after that is no longer matched; retire
a lead with `--close` before spawning its successor.

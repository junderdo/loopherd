# D71

## Decision

A watch says `<herd> out of work` when a herd isn't paused, claimable is
0, unready is 0 (or the herd has no `backlog`), and no worker is open.

- **Who prints it.** A lead's watch, for its herd, in its 60s round of
  checks. The shepherd's watch, also every 60s, for each herd it reads
  that has a herd file and no open lead or worker: all of them, or those
  named on `loopherd watch <herd>`. A led herd is left to its lead. The
  shepherd's round runs first on a re-armed watch's first quiet poll, and
  never on a poll that already has lines to print, so a slow `source`
  can't hold them back.
- **Once per streak.** The key stays in the watch's fired file
  (`watch-<lead>.fired`, or the shepherd's `watch.fired`) until claimable
  or unready rises above 0 or a worker opens, which appends `<herd> out of
  work ended`. The shepherd's watch ends the streak of any herd with an
  open lead or worker, in its scope or not; claimable rising is seen only
  for herds in scope. A paused herd prints nothing and keeps its streak, as
  `running low` does (D60). A failing or cut-off `source` or `backlog`
  leaves the streak as it is: a lead's watch prints `failing`, and the
  shepherd's warns on stderr once per watch.
- **Who acts.** The lead reports it with one `question` (pause the herd, or
  file and approve work) and ends its turn. The shepherd relays it, or
  tells the human for an unled herd. Nothing pauses a herd automatically.
- **Check timeout.** The shepherd's watch reads the check timeout only when
  it has an unled herd to check, and warns rather than failing when the
  setting is bad.

This amends D60's "no herd checks in the shepherd's watch": the shepherd's
watch runs an unled herd's `source` and `backlog`, but no member checks.

LH-262 settled it.

## Why

`running low` needs unready tasks and `drained` needs a pause, so an
unpaused herd with an empty queue and no worker printed nothing: the lead
idled on its watch and neither the shepherd nor the human heard. On
2026-11-18 loopherd-cards ran out of work and the human noticed only by
reading the lead's pane.

An unled herd has no lead watch to notice, so the shepherd's watch must.
It runs only the queue commands, and only for herds with no lead or worker
open, which an idle herd left unpaused costs on each re-arm; pausing it
stops that. The line is keyed without counts so
a herd left idle on purpose wakes no one twice. A pause keeps the streak so
pausing and resuming an idle herd doesn't re-announce it.

# D69

## Decision

Each ledger entry records `tab_id`, the herdr tab holding its pane: spawn
records the tab it creates, revive the tab it starts the member in (a new
one or a reused shell pane's), and restart the live agent's tab and pane.
Revive also records the workspace id its label has now, since a herdr
restart can renumber workspaces. An entry written before this has no
`tab_id` and still loads.

The herdr plugin hooks `tab.closed` and `workspace.closed` to
`loopherd herdr-event-hook`. On `tab.closed` it records `gone` for every
non-retired member on `HERDR_SOCKET_PATH` whose `tab_id` is the event's
`data.tab_id`; on `workspace.closed`, for every such member whose
`workspace` is `data.workspace_id`. Each goes through the same
once-per-start rule as `pane.closed` (D66), so closing the last tab, which
fires both events, records one `gone`. The hook still never calls herdr.

Limits:

- An entry with no `tab_id`, until a revive starts it or a restart, gets
  no `gone` from `tab.closed`; `workspace.closed` and a running watch's
  poll still cover it. A revive that finds the member alive records
  nothing.
- A member's pane moved to another tab or workspace (`herdr pane move`)
  keeps its pane id but not its recorded tab or workspace: closing the old
  one records a false `gone`, and closing the new one records none.
  Following `pane.moved` is LH-257.
- Workspace ids, and a new workspace's tab ids, can be reused after a herdr
  restart. A member whose recorded ids were reused, with no revive since,
  is recorded `gone` when the reused tab or workspace closes.

LH-253 settled it.

## Why

herdr 0.9.5 closing a tab emits neither `pane.exited` nor `pane.closed`,
and every member gets its own tab at spawn, so closing that tab was the
usual way a member went unseen between watches. Recording the tab in the
ledger lets the hook map the event to members from its own state, as it
does panes, rather than asking herdr in a hook that must stay fast and
offline.

# D98

## Decision

A lead's herdr-touching loopherd commands run in a **broker**, one per
lead, outside its sandbox. In a lead's pane, sandboxed or hook-only,
loopherd hands the operation to the broker and calls no herdr itself.
The shepherd's commands, and members of unled herds, keep calling herdr
directly. Builds on D89.

- **The gate is a whole loopherd operation.** A request names an
  operation and its arguments, never a herdr command. A herdr-command filter
  can't tell an honest feed from a hostile `agent start`; an operation can
  be checked against the herd.
- **Lifetime.** `loopherd lead` starts the broker detached, in a session
  of its own, as `restart --self` starts its waiter (`loopherd broker
  <lead>`, hidden). `revive`, startup revive included, starts it for
  each unretired lead whose broker isn't running, and fails when it can't.
  The broker exits once the ledger has its lead retired or gone. A flock
  on `<config dir>/broker/<lead>.lock` keeps it to one per lead: a second
  broker exits at once.
- **Identity is fixed at launch.** The broker takes its lead's name from
  its command line and its herd from `sandbox/<lead>.lead.json`, which
  `lead` writes into the config dir, where the lead can't write. It never
  reads either from a request, `HERDR_PANE_ID` or the ledger.
- **The transport is files, not a socket.** On Linux and WSL2 Claude
  Code's sandbox allows every Unix socket or none, so a lead that ends up
  with none can't reach a socket broker. The lead writes each request as
  one JSON file, renamed into `<state dir>/broker/<lead>/requests/`. The
  broker writes the reply to `<config dir>/broker/<lead>/replies/`, which
  the lead can read but not write, then removes the request. A request
  that isn't a regular file under 1 MiB holding exactly one known
  operation is refused. One whose file name can't name a reply is only
  removed. A panic answers that request as failed. Replies left unread
  are pruned after 10 minutes.
- **Reach.** The broker carries out an operation only on its own herd's
  unretired workers. It refuses, naming why, the shepherd, the lead
  itself, a member of another herd, a member in no herd, another lead,
  and a name with no unretired entry. `restart --self` and report may
  name the lead itself.
- **Failure is loud.** The lead's command checks the broker's lock
  first. With no broker running it fails at once, naming the broker and
  `loopherd revive --name <lead>`. It also fails as soon as the broker
  stops without answering. `loopherd doctor` fails for each unretired
  lead with no broker.
- **The lead in a pane** is the one whose sandboxed Claude is an
  ancestor process (D89's `calling_lead`), else the lead the ledger has
  in `$HERDR_PANE_ID` on `$HERDR_SOCKET_PATH`. Finding it asks herdr
  nothing.

Operations routed so far: `tell`. Feed, add, prompt, restart,
`restart --self`, retire `--close`, release, status, the watch's polls,
notifications, pane labels and a new `read` follow under LH-301, as does
`after_merge` running in the broker.

## Why

herdr has no auth, scoping or read-only mode, so whoever holds its socket
holds every pane. D89 named the socket as the sandbox's biggest gap: a
hijacked lead could `herdr agent start` a process outside its sandbox, or
prompt and read any pane. Moving every herdr call a lead needs into a
process the lead can't change lets a follow-up card take the lead's
sockets away. Until then sockets stay open, so a missed operation breaks
nothing.

One broker per lead, rather than a per-user daemon, the shepherd's watch
or herdr's plugin, keeps each broker's identity fixed at its launch and
its failures to one herd; the human chose it.

## What it still depends on

- **The ledger (LH-298, LH-307).** The broker reads the ledger to learn a
  herd's workers, and a lead can still write the ledger and the whole
  state dir. Until LH-298 denies it the ledger, a lead can forge an entry
  that puts any pane's agent in its herd. Until LH-307 narrows per-agent
  paths, a lead can also drop requests into another lead's request
  folder, which that lead's broker answers within that herd's reach.
- **Unsandboxed workers (LH-284).** `tell` and `add` text reaches a
  worker that runs outside any sandbox, so a lead can still have a worker
  do what its own sandbox refuses.
- **Its lock.** A lead can open the broker's lock read-only and hold it,
  so no broker starts for it. That only stops its own commands.

# D49

## Decision

A lead's watch prints `<herd> failing  <what> "<command>": <last stderr
line>` when the herd's source or a worker's `done` or `merged` check
fails, once per failure streak, and `<herd> recovered  <what>` the first
time it passes again. Both are keyed in `watch-<lead>.fired` like the other
events; a `recovered` key forgets its `failing` one, so the next failure
prints again. A check or source cut off by the round's 60s budget, before
or while it runs, is neither. The stderr line loses ANSI escapes and
control characters and is cut to 200 characters. A check that can't be
rendered, such as one for a worker with no recorded params, names the
check as the herd file writes it.

A worker that retires mid-streak ends its streak silently: no `recovered`
prints, and its `failing` key stays in the file, where it matches nothing
again, since a retired name is never reused within the herd.

## Why

A failing source or check used to print nothing, so a lead that only
watched never learned that origin was unreachable until something ran
`feed`. Keying the streak in the fired file, not in memory, stops every
re-armed watch from waking on the same failure. Printing the recovery as
well as forgetting it tells the lead its last `failing` line is stale.
A deadline-cut command says nothing about its own health, and counting it
would wake the lead every slow round. The lead retires the member itself
or sees feed do it, so a silent end leaves it no stale belief.

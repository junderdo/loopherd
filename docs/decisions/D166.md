# D166

## Decision

A lead's watch ends once the Claude it runs under is gone (LH-528). At
start it takes the nearest Claude above it in its own process lineage. At
the top of each poll, once that process (pid and start time) is no longer
live, the watch fails with `<lead>'s Claude (pid <n>) is gone, so its watch
ends`, which releases `watch-<lead>.lock`. A lead's watch with no Claude
above it, and the shepherd's watch, are unchanged.

## Why

A lead's watch runs as a background task of its Claude. `/exit` (stopping
tasks), SIGHUP and SIGTERM all stop it with Claude, but a Claude killed
outright (SIGKILL, a crash, OOM) leaves the watch orphaned and still holding
the lock. `loopherd revive` then sees `(watch running)` and doesn't prompt
the re-arm, so the revived lead sits unwatched while the orphan's output
goes nowhere. Ending the watch where the fault starts was chosen over
having revive judge a lock holder stale and kill it. That second approach
has its own card.

Claude Code's `/exit` option "Move to background and exit" also ends the
pane's Claude process, so the watch ends with it there too.

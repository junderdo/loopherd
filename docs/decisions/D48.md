# D48

## Decision

The lead hook (D17) keeps its hand-written lexer and fails closed. It doesn't
switch to a bash parser crate. LH-165 denied the forms its card listed,
and their neighbours its review found:

- `function`, `coproc` (named or not) and `trap` bodies are read as
  commands.
- `ionice`, `taskset` and `flock` are wrappers, and `flock FILE -c` is read
  as a script.
- gh, git and shell aliases are denied whatever the switches
  (`Switch::Alias`, listed in D17).
- A shell, `source` or `.` whose script comes from its input is unreadable,
  unless a `<<` heredoc feeds it.
- So is a separator, blank or parenthesis inside an assignment's `name[…]`
  or an array's `[…]`.

D29's Limits names the forms the hook still can't read.

Revisit this when a crate meets three conditions. It must be bash-faithful.
It must fail loudly on input it doesn't parse. And it must give a recursive
AST through words, arithmetic and `${…}` operands. Use it as a second
opinion: deny when it errs or finds a command the lexer didn't.

## Why

[research/bash-parser-crates.md](../research/bash-parser-crates.md)
compared brush-parser, shuck-parser, rable, yash-syntax and
tree-sitter-bash against real bash on 97 inputs; conch-parser is archived
and POSIX-only. Every crate that parses
bash silently missed at least one input where bash runs the gated command:

- brush-parser 0.4.0 swallows a heredoc after `((…<<…))` into arithmetic
  text. That is the class of bug that LH-132's ten review rounds kept
  finding in the hand lexer, and only brush's unreleased `main` fixes it.
- shuck-parser and rable miss nested escaped backticks and
  `$((cmd); cmd)`.
- tree-sitter-bash and yash-syntax mis-parse `coproc` and `time`. yash
  also splits words on Unicode whitespace.

None of them expands words. So brace expansion, `$'…'`, wrappers, sinks,
`bash -c`/`eval` recursion and the cargo-alias tracking stay hand-written
either way. A parser would replace only the lexer, about 750 of
`tool_hook.rs`'s 1,750 non-test lines, and bring its own silent gaps, so it is no safer than the fail-closed lexer it
would replace. The fail-closed rules already denied the nested-arithmetic
inputs from PR #115's last review; the subscript ones needed the rule
above.

# D55

## Decision

A paused herd with nothing left in flight is an end state a watch reports
and exits on, instead of re-arming over it.

- A lead's watch prints `<herd> drained` and exits 0 when its herd is paused
  and has no open (non-retired) worker. A worker that is done but not yet
  retired is still open.
- The shepherd's watch prints `all drained` and exits 0 when it reads at
  least one herd, every herd it reads is paused with no open worker, and no
  member it reads (leads, members of unled herds, members in no herd) has
  work in flight. A member has work in flight while herdr shows it working,
  blocked or unknown, or while its last stop since its last start left
  shells, subagents, crons or unknown background running, or while its
  mailbox can't be read. A gone member has none.
- `drained` prints alone, only on a poll nothing else woke, so the watch's
  other lines are handled first and the next watch exits at once.
- The pause is read on the poll that decides, after the members, so a
  resume between polls never yields `drained`. An unreadable pause file,
  which `feed` and the claimable check count as paused, does not count as
  drained: exiting a watch on a file that can't be read would drop events.
- Neither `pause` nor `resume` changes. After `loopherd resume` the
  shepherd re-arms its watch by hand and tells a stopped lead to re-arm.

LH-214 built it.

## Why

`watch` read a herd's pause only to skip `claimable`. After a full drain
nothing marked the herd as finished, so the lead and the shepherd each
re-armed an hourly watch over a herd that would never wake them again,
holding sessions open with nothing to do.

Requiring at least one herd keeps a shepherd's watch armed before its first
spawn. Counting a stop's background work as in flight keeps an idle member
whose cron or shell can still wake it from being declared drained.

# D32

## Decision

The human is notified outside the shepherd's pane when something waits on
them: a `question` report or a member going `blocked`. `config.toml` takes a
`[notify]` table: `enabled` (default `false`), `kinds` (`question`,
`blocked`; default both), `herdr` (default `true`) and an optional `command`.
With no table or `enabled = false`, nothing is sent. Unknown keys, kinds or
placeholders are an error that `loopherd herds` reports, enabled or not, and a
broken table sends nothing (the watch warns). It is read apart from the
context-limit keys, so a broken `[notify]`, whatever its shape, never costs
a member its limit.

**Sender.** The watch that prints the event sends it, after printing and
before marking it read: a lead's watch for its workers, the shepherd's for
the rest (D17's readers). A lead's watch also sends the lead's own questions
and blocks as it first sees them, without printing them or marking them
read, so a lead running with no shepherd session still reaches the human;
the shepherd's watch still prints and sends them too. No daemon.

Each event is sent by whichever watch marks it first. A question is marked
in `reports/<member>.notified` (keyed by its mailbox time, under a flock),
so a watch that re-reads it (a wake never marked read, a herd gaining a lead
mid-wake, the lead's and the shepherd's watch both) sends nothing more. A
block is marked by creating `reports/<member>.blocked`, which any watch
removes when it sees the member neither blocked nor unknown, so the next
block is sent. A watch sees a block only as a change (D15), so a member
already blocked when every watch starts is never notified.

Two more gaps are accepted. A question read with a plain `loopherd reports`
before any watch prints it is never notified: the human is already reading.
A lead that relays a worker's question as its own sends a second
notification for it.

**Senders.** herdr's toast is `herdr notification show "<herd>: <member>"
--body=<text> --sound request` (`--body=`, so a text starting with `-`
isn't taken for a flag), run with the watch's own environment so it
reaches the socket the watch reads. `command` is words split on whitespace
(quotes refused; an array gives exact arguments), with `{herd}`, `{member}`,
`{kind}` and `{text}` filled within each argument, never through a shell.
`<text>` is the question's first non-blank line, cut to 200 characters, with
`[redacted]` for: words that look like tokens (known prefixes such as
`ghp_`, long mixed letter-and-digit runs); `key=value` and `key:value`
words, quoted or not (`{"token":"abc"}`), whose key is a secret key such as
`password`, `token`, `auth`, `session` or `cookie` as a whole word or after
`_`/`-` (`x-api-key=`, not `monkey=`); the word after `password:`,
`"password":`, `--token`, `Bearer` or an identifier such as `GITHUB_TOKEN`
or `API-KEY`; both words after `Authorization:` and a scheme (`Bearer`,
`Basic`, `Digest`, `Token`); and the whole of a quoted value. After a bare
key word (`password`, `the token is`), the next word goes only if it looks
like a secret: it has a digit, or is 8 or more characters with no vowel, so
"the auth flow" and "rotate the token now" stay whole. A URL's
`user:password@` is hidden. For `blocked` the text is `blocked`. Redaction
is best-effort: a secret in a shape it doesn't know reaches the toast and
the command, including a short flag's value (`-p hunter2`), a short JWT, and
a plain value after a key in prose (`the key is abc`), so `kinds` or
`herdr = false` is the way to keep questions off a shared screen.

**Detached.** Each send is its own `loopherd notify-run` (hidden), in its
own process group with no stdio, so the watch neither waits for it nor takes
it down on exit. It runs the command within 30s, killing its group at the
timeout. A watch whose binary was replaced (a reinstall) starts the
`loopherd` on PATH instead. A failure (`notify-run` or its command can't
start, non-zero exit, timeout) appends one `note`
to the member's mailbox, then stays quiet for that sender (herdr or command)
until it next succeeds, tracked by `reports/.notify-<sender>.failed`.

Out of scope: answering from a notification, notifying on `done`, `note` or
stops, a desktop notification library (it can't reach Windows from WSL),
and a mute switch beyond editing `[notify]`.

## Why

A question or a blocking dialog waited until the human looked at the
shepherd's pane, and leads running unattended make those waits longer.
herdr's toast needs no setup but shows only while herdr is on screen; the
command reaches the human anywhere (a Windows toast from WSL,
`notify-send`, an ntfy or Pushover push). The mechanism and sender were the
human's call on LH-114, including a lead's watch sending the lead's own
events: a lead's merge question is the case that most needs to reach the
human, and it often runs with no shepherd session.

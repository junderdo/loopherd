# D30

## Decision

`loopherd stats` reads only the ledger and the mailboxes. A retired worker's
row runs from its ledger `created` (started) to its `retired_at`: time to
its first `done` report, the number of `done` reports (review rounds), time
from the last `done` to retirement (waiting on merge), and the total. Only
`done` reports between `created` and `retired_at` count, so a reused name's
earlier mailbox entries don't. `retired_at` has whole seconds while mailbox
times have nanoseconds, so a `done` anywhere in the second of retirement
counts. A value that can't be computed (no `done`, no `retired_at` in an old
ledger) shows as `-` and is left out of the herd's medians and maxima. Leads
and entries with no herd are left out; unretired workers are listed apart
with their time so far and aren't summarized. The rounds median, like the
time medians, covers only members with at least one `done`: a worker retired
without one (abandoned) keeps its row with 0 rounds and still counts toward
the total column. `--since` keeps members started on or after the given
time: a date means midnight UTC, and an RFC 3339 time needs `Z` or a numeric
offset, which is honored; anything else is refused.

## Why

The numbers are for judging whether leads can be trusted with more autonomy
(LH-110), which is a question about workers' cycles: how long a card takes,
how many review rounds it needs, and how long its PR waits on the human. A
lead runs for the life of its herd, so its times would swamp the medians.
Starting time is `created`, not the mailbox's first `start`, because revive
appends further `start` entries and older members may have none. An
abandoned worker's zero rounds would say nothing about review, only drag the
median down. Filtering by start keeps a member's row the same whichever day
it is read.

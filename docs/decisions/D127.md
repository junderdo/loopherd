# D127

Amends D32.

## Decision

The shepherd's watch checks, each poll, that every unretired lead on its
herdr has a broker running, and prints `<lead> no broker` once per outage
(LH-368). The streak is keyed by the lead's spawn in `watch.fired` and ends
on the first poll that finds the broker running.

`[notify]` gains a `no_broker` kind, in the default `kinds`. The lead's own
watch sends it for itself, and the shepherd's watch sends what it prints;
a `reports/<lead>.no-broker` mark keeps it to one send per outage across
both, and any watch that sees the broker running removes it.

Watches only report: neither starts a broker. `loopherd broker-restart
<lead>` starts one, and `revive_on_startup` starts them after herdr restores
a session.

## Why

After a host restart herdr restored a lead's pane and Claude, but with
`revive_on_startup` off nothing started its broker. With no broker the
lead's `feed`, `status` and `report` all fail, so it couldn't ask for
help and sat idle until the shepherd read its screen. A broker that dies
while herdr stays up has the same effect. Starting one from a watch is left
out, since D106 keeps broker control out of a lead's pane.

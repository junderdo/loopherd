# D85

## Decision

While a lead with `merge = false` and `merge_guard = "hook"` runs, its watch
tells the human of every pull request merged into its repo's default branch,
through `[notify]` as kind `merge_alert`. Amends D32: `merge_alert` joins
`kinds` and its default.

- **Config.** `[lead] merge_guard` names how a lead with `merge` off is kept
  from merging. `hook` (the default) is the PreToolUse hook alone; it is the
  only value for now, and an unknown one fails the herd file. A lead with
  `merge` on, from its `[lead]` or `[lead_defaults]`, gets no alert: its
  merges are in its reports.
- **Who asks.** The lead's own watch, with its other checks, and only while
  `[notify]` is enabled with `merge_alert` in `kinds`, so a herd that sends
  nothing never calls gh. A table that lists its kinds sends it only when it
  lists `merge_alert`, which isn't `merged`: a lead's watch already prints
  that for a worker's expected merge.
- **Window.** The watch keeps `<lead>.merges` in the mailboxes directory:
  the lead's ledger `created`, the window's start, the last lookup's time
  and the cached default branch. A lead's first lookup, or one finding the
  state of another lead by that name (a different `created`), writes a
  baseline window starting now and sends nothing, so neither an upgrade nor
  a predecessor floods the human with old merges. The baseline starts 5
  seconds back, for a local clock ahead of GitHub's. A file that can't be
  parsed, such as one holding a bad time, starts a new baseline too. Retire
  removes the file.
- **Lookups.** At most one a minute across all the lead's watches, so a
  re-arm doesn't ask again. Each runs `gh pr list --state merged --base
  <branch> --search "merged:>=<since> sort:updated-desc" --limit 100`
  within 10s (`gh repo view` for the branch, again each hour, so a renamed
  default branch is picked up). `<since>` is the
  window's start, or later when the lead's mailbox records a `start` after
  its last `gone` since it was created (a revive): merges while a lead was
  recorded down are skipped. A revive with no `gone` recorded, such as a
  server restart no watch saw, skips nothing. After a lookup the window
  starts 15 minutes before it, so a merge GitHub's search indexes late is
  still found and a re-armed watch catches up on what merged meanwhile.
- **Stale windows.** A lookup whose window starts over an hour before the
  usual 15 minutes, as after `[notify]` or `merge = false` was off or no
  watch ran for a while, sends one line, `<N> merges while unwatched`
  (`100 or more` at gh's limit), instead of a toast per merge, and starts
  a new baseline. A window kept by a successful lookup never gets that old,
  and a failing one keeps its start, so a long outage also ends in one line.
- **Clock jumps.** A last lookup time ahead of now (a clock that jumped
  back, common on WSL2) counts as due rather than holding lookups off. A
  window start ahead of now is read as 15 minutes ago.
- **Wakes first.** A due lookup waits for a round of the watch with nothing
  to report, so gh's up to 20s never holds back a wake.
- **Once per merge.** A send marks `merge_alert <PR URL>` in the lead's
  `<lead>.notified`, as a question marks its time, so a PR listed by several
  lookups is sent once. The text is `#<number> <URL>`.
- **Failures.** A failing lookup, or a failure to read the lead's mailbox
  or save the window, sends nothing and warns on the watch's stderr once
  per streak within that watch; a re-armed watch warns again on its first
  failure.
- **Not covered.** Pushes straight to the default branch (the hook guards
  `gh pr merge` and the merge API, not pushes; branch protection is the
  human's backstop), merges after the lead's watch stops for good (a
  drained lead), and more than 100 merges in one window short of stale
  (about 75 minutes).

## Why

A hook-guarded lead runs with the human's GitHub credentials, so a hijacked
lead could get a merge past the hook. The human is the only one who should
merge in such a herd, so a ping per merge lets them spot one they didn't
make. Which identity merged can't be told apart (same credentials), so every
merge pings. The lead's watch is the cheapest place that already runs for
the lead's lifetime and already notifies the human directly (D32's
own-question path); the shepherd's watch may not be running. A later value
of `merge_guard` that keeps the credentials away from the lead can skip the
alert.

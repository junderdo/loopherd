# D85

## Decision

While a lead with `merge = false` and `merge_guard = "hook"` runs, its watch
tells the human of every pull request merged into its repo's default branch,
through `[notify]` as kind `merge_alert`. Amends D32: `merge_alert` joins
`kinds` and its default.

- **Config.** `[lead] merge_guard` names how a lead with `merge` off is kept
  from merging. `hook` (the default) is the PreToolUse hook alone; D95 adds
  `credentials`, which gets no alert, and an unknown one fails the herd file. A lead with
  `merge` on, from its `[lead]` or `[lead_defaults]`, gets no alert: its
  merges are in its reports.
- **Who asks.** The lead's own watch, with its other checks, and only while
  `[notify]` is enabled with `merge_alert` in `kinds`, so a herd that sends
  nothing never calls gh. A table that lists its kinds sends it only when it
  lists `merge_alert`, which isn't `merged`: a lead's watch already prints
  that for a worker's expected merge.
- **Window.** The watch keeps `<lead>.merges` in the mailboxes directory:
  the lead's ledger `created`, the window's start, the last lookup's time
  and the cached default branch. A lead's first lookup, or one finding the
  state of another lead by that name (a different `created`), writes a
  baseline window starting now and sends nothing, so neither an upgrade nor
  a predecessor floods the human with old merges. The baseline starts 5
  seconds back, for a local clock ahead of GitHub's. A file that can't be
  parsed, such as one holding a bad time, starts a new baseline too. Retire
  removes the file.
- **Lookups.** At most one a minute across all the lead's watches, so a
  re-arm doesn't ask again. Each runs `gh pr list --state merged --base
  <branch> --search "merged:>=<since> sort:updated-desc" --limit 100`
  within 10s (`gh repo view` for the branch, again each hour, so a renamed
  default branch is picked up; a failing refresh keeps the cached branch).
  `<since>` is the window's start, or later when the lead's mailbox records
  a `start` after its last `gone` since it was created (a revive): merges
  while a lead was recorded down are skipped. A revive with no `gone`
  recorded, such as a server restart no watch saw, skips nothing. After a
  lookup the window starts 15 minutes before it, so a merge GitHub's search
  indexes late is still found and a re-armed watch catches up on what merged
  meanwhile.
- **Stale windows.** A lookup whose window starts over an hour before the
  usual 15 minutes (75 minutes back), as after `[notify]` or `merge = false`
  was off or no watch ran for a while, sends one line, `<N> merges while
  unwatched` (`<N> or more` at gh's limit of 100), instead of a toast per
  merge. Once the window is saved the lookup marks its merges sent, all
  under one lock, and `<N>` counts those it was first to mark: a merge
  already sent isn't counted or later sent alone, and two watches looking
  up at once send one line between them, never a part each. Nothing is sent
  when it marked none. A window that can't be saved marks none, so the next
  lookup counts them again. When the marks can't be written, nothing is
  sent, the watch warns as for a failed lookup, and the window goes back to
  its old start, so the next lookup counts those merges again; a mark that
  was written all the same may then be left out of that count. If putting
  the window back fails as well, as on a full disk, it stays moved and
  those merges are missed; the warning then names the save. A lookup that
  finds no merges touches no marks. The window otherwise starts 15 minutes
  back as usual, so a merge indexed late is still sent. While lookups keep
  succeeding a window never gets that old, and a failing one keeps its
  start, so a long outage also ends in one line.
- **Clock jumps.** A last lookup time ahead of now (a clock that jumped
  back, common on WSL2) counts as due rather than holding lookups off. A
  revive recorded ahead of now is ignored, and a window start ahead of now
  is read as 15 minutes ago, so no saved start is ever ahead of now.
- **Wakes first.** A due lookup waits for a round of the watch with nothing
  to report, so gh's up to 20s doesn't hold back a wake. Once the last
  lookup is 15 minutes old it runs on a waking round too, so watches that
  wake on every first round can't starve it.
- **Once per merge.** A send marks `merge_alert <PR URL>` in the lead's
  `<lead>.notified`, as a question marks its time, so a PR listed by several
  lookups is sent once. The text is `#<number> <URL>`.
- **Failures.** A failing lookup, or a failure to read the lead's mailbox,
  save the window or mark a summary's merges, sends nothing and warns on
  the watch's stderr once per streak within that watch; a re-armed watch
  warns again on its first failure. A streak ends only with a lookup whose
  window was saved and whose summary, if any, was marked, and an
  unsaved window keeps its start, so its merges are found again. While it
  can't be saved, each of the lead's watches looks up once a minute, as the
  shared limit lives in that file. A watch also keeps its own last lookup
  in memory, so the 15-minute wait on a waking round holds within it; a
  re-armed watch that wakes on its first round looks up.
- **Not covered.** Pushes straight to the default branch (the hook guards
  `gh pr merge` and the merge API, not pushes; branch protection is the
  human's backstop), merges after the lead's watch stops for good (a
  drained lead), and more than 100 merges in one window short of stale
  (about 75 minutes).

## Why

A hook-guarded lead runs with the human's GitHub credentials, so a hijacked
lead could get a merge past the hook. The human is the only one who should
merge in such a herd, so a ping per merge lets them spot one they didn't
make. Which identity merged can't be told apart (same credentials), so every
merge pings. The lead's watch is the cheapest place that already runs for
the lead's lifetime and already notifies the human directly (D32's
own-question path); the shepherd's watch may not be running. A later value
of `merge_guard` that keeps the credentials away from the lead can skip the
alert.
